02 / how to measure

From information need to decision

The measurement information model from ISO/IEC 15939 still works. Start from the decision, not from the data you happen to have.

01

Information need

Which decision, by whom? “Can we release on Friday?”

02

Base measures

Raw counts collected automatically: open defects, failed builds.

03

Derived measures

Combine them: defect inflow per week, failure rate.

04

Indicator

Add decision criteria: green, yellow, red, with agreed thresholds.

05

Decision

Show it where the stakeholder already looks, and act on it.

06

Review

Retire indicators nobody acts on. Recalibrate thresholds.

Actionable over interesting

If nobody can say what they would do when the number changes, it is not a metric. It is trivia.

Telemetry first, surveys second

Collect automatically from pipelines, repos and IDEs. Use surveys as an occasional temperature check.

Pair speed with stability

Every speed metric needs a quality counterpart. Faster cycle time with doubled rework is not progress.

Beware vanity metrics

“Lines generated by AI” or “prompts per day” go up and to the right and tell you nothing about value.

Trends, not snapshots

A single value invites argument. A trend with thresholds invites a decision.

Give the metric team a mandate

Measurement programs last when they have a formal mandate, sit inside product development and keep a stable team.
